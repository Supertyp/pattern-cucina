  - pattern: buying-hard-cheeses
    note: Take a chunk of Parmigiano Reggiano aged about 24 months or less. Younger cheese is milkier and better for eating in pieces, while very old cheese is for grating.
  - pattern: buying-fresh-cheeses
    note: Buy one burrata or a few bocconcini on the day. Keep it cold until the last minute, and tear it open at the table so the creamy centre spills out.
  - pattern: storing-cheese
    note: If you shop the day before, wrap the hard and semi-soft cheeses in paper and keep the fresh cheese in its liquid. Take out only what goes on the board, about 30 minutes ahead.
  - pattern: grilling-vegetables
    note: Grill an eggplant and two zucchini in 5 to 8 mm slices on a dry, hot pan, and char a red capsicum whole. Dress them with oil, garlic, mint or parsley and a few drops of vinegar at least 30 minutes before serving.
  - pattern: using-day-old-bread
    note: Turn yesterday's loaf into crostini. Slice it thinly, brush with oil and toast in a 180 °C oven (160 °C fan-forced) for about 8 to 10 minutes, until golden and crisp at the edges.
  - pattern: building-an-antipasto-board
    note: Group each item in its own cluster on a big board, with the oily vegetables and olives in small bowls so they don't soak the bread. Break the Parmigiano into rough chunks and drape the prosciutto in loose folds.
  - pattern: bread-at-the-italian-table
    note: Put a basket of sliced fresh bread and the crostini on the board itself or beside it, with grissini if you like, but no butter or bowls of dipping oil. Here bread belongs with the salumi.
  - pattern: serving-wine-at-the-right-temperature
    note: Salty meats and cheese love bubbles and acidity. Serve a dry Prosecco or a crisp white at 6 to 8 °C, or a sparkling red Lambrusco slightly chilled, in an ice bucket on the table.
  - pattern: aperitivo-and-digestivo
    note: If a meal follows, the board doubles as the aperitivo, so keep drinks to one glass and offer an Italian non-alcoholic bitter or sparkling water with orange alongside.

## About the dish

A *tagliere* (literally a cutting board) of salumi and cheese is served all over Italy, and each region fills it with its own hams, salami and cheeses. When it's right, the meats are thin and silky, the fat translucent rather than waxy, the cheeses smell of milk and hay, and there's enough contrast (sweet, salty, sharp, creamy) that every bite tastes different. It should open the appetite, not end it. How lavish to make it is a matter of taste and occasion: before a full meal, Italians tend to keep it modest.

## What you need

- 120 g Prosciutto di Parma or San Daniele, sliced paper-thin
- 100 g salami or finocchiona, sliced thinly
- 80 g Mortadella Bologna, sliced thinly
- 80 g Parmigiano Reggiano in a piece
- 80 g semi-soft cheese, such as Asiago or a young pecorino
- 1 burrata (about 125 g) or 125 g bocconcini
- 1 eggplant, 2 zucchini and 1 red capsicum
- 60 ml extra virgin olive oil, 1 garlic clove, a few mint or parsley leaves and red wine vinegar for the vegetables
- 150 g olives and a jar of artichokes in oil
- 1 day-old loaf for crostini, plus fresh bread and grissini
- Fruit in season, such as figs or melon
- Equipment: a ridged grill pan, a large board or platter, a short pointed cheese knife and small bowls

## Where it usually goes wrong

- **Sweaty, waxy meat and cheese.** Served straight from the fridge, then left out too long. Time it, and put it away within 2 hours (see [Building an antipasto board](../../patterns/building-an-antipasto-board/)).
- **Nobody eats dinner.** The board was sized as a meal. Weigh it out by the person (see [Buying guanciale, pancetta and prosciutto](../../patterns/buying-cured-meats/)).
- **Greasy, pale vegetables.** They were oiled before grilling instead of after (see [Grilling vegetables](../../patterns/grilling-vegetables/)).
- **A guest who can't eat it.** Cold cured meats and soft cheeses such as burrata aren't advised for pregnant women and others at higher risk of *Listeria*, so add plenty of grilled vegetables, Parmigiano and crostini for them (see [Buying mozzarella, burrata and ricotta](../../patterns/buying-fresh-cheeses/)).
