## About the dish

The *bistecca alla fiorentina* is Florence's great steak: a thick T-bone or porterhouse cut from the loin, with the fillet on one side of the bone and the sirloin on the other. Traditionally it comes from Chianina or other Tuscan cattle, is cut about three fingers thick, and is grilled over wood or charcoal embers. When it's right, it has a dark, salty crust and a rare, red, tender centre, and it's shared at the table, sliced, with beans, salad or roast potatoes. Florentines are firm about cooking it rare, and there's lively debate over when to salt and whether to oil it. A popular story traces its name to English visitors asking for "beef steak", but treat that as legend.

## What you need

- 1 T-bone steak, about 5 cm thick (about 1.3 kg)
- 300 g dried cannellini beans
- A few sage leaves and 1 garlic clove for the beans
- Coarse sea salt and freshly ground black pepper
- Good Tuscan extra virgin olive oil, for the beans and the table
- Lemon wedges (optional)
- Equipment: a charcoal grill (or a heavy griddle pan for a thinner steak), long tongs, a probe thermometer, a sharp knife and a large board with a juice groove

## Where it usually goes wrong

- **Burnt outside, cold and raw at the bone.** The fire was too fierce with no cooler zone, or the steak wasn't stood on its bone. See [Grilling a thick steak](../../patterns/grilling-steak/).
- **Overcooked fillet.** The small fillet side sat over the hottest coals. Probe both sides, as in [Cooking meat by temperature](../../patterns/cooking-meat-by-temperature/).
- **Juices all over the board.** It was cut straight off the grill. Rest it first, as in [Resting meat](../../patterns/resting-meat/).
- **Beans with chalky centres or burst skins.** They were old, boiled hard or cooked too briefly. Buy from a busy shop, keep them at a shiver and taste five beans, as in [Cooking dried beans, chickpeas and lentils](../../patterns/cooking-dried-legumes/).
