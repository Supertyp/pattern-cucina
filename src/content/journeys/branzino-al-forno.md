## About the dish

Baked whole fish with potatoes is a family dish all along Italy's coasts. *Branzino* is the northern name for the European sea bass; around Rome and further south it's usually called *spigola*. Most sold today is farmed. The dish is simple, so it depends on a very fresh fish and accurate timing: the flesh should be moist, white and just flaking off the bone, the skin lightly golden, and the potatoes underneath tender and crisp at the edges, soaked in oil and fish juices. Each coast has its own touches, such as olives and capers, cherry tomatoes or a splash of white wine. Some cooks prefer the fish *al sale*, baked in a salt crust, which steams it gently instead.

## What you need

- 2 whole sea bass (about 700 g each), or another firm white fish of similar size, scaled and gutted
- 1 kg potatoes
- 4 tablespoons extra virgin olive oil, plus extra to serve
- 2 sprigs rosemary
- 1 lemon, sliced, plus wedges to serve
- A small bunch of flat-leaf parsley
- 1 garlic clove
- A handful of cherry tomatoes or olives (optional)
- 4 handfuls of salad leaves
- Salt and pepper
- Equipment: a large roasting tray, baking paper, a fish slice or two spatulas, and a probe thermometer if you have one

## Where it usually goes wrong

- **Dry, woolly fish.** It baked as long as the potatoes. Give the potatoes their head start and time the fish by thickness, as in [Cooking whole fish](../../patterns/cooking-whole-fish/).
- **Pale, undercooked potatoes.** The slices were thick or crowded. Use one layer and roast first, as in [Roasting vegetables](../../patterns/roasting-vegetables/).
- **A strong fishy smell.** The fish wasn't fresh. See [Buying fresh fish and shellfish](../../patterns/buying-fresh-seafood/).
- **Bones on every plate.** The fillets were cut off in pieces. Lift the whole skeleton out from the tail, as in [Cooking whole fish](../../patterns/cooking-whole-fish/).
