    note: Use 4 litres of water and only about 25 g of salt, roughly 6 g per litre. Guanciale, pecorino and the pasta water itself all bring salt to this sauce.
  - pattern: cooking-pasta-al-dente

    note: Loosen the egg mixture with a few spoonfuls of warm pasta water. For pregnant women, young children, older people or anyone with weakened immunity, use pasteurised eggs or stir the sauce over a water bath to 72 °C.

    note: Toss the pasta in the fat with a splash of water over the heat for a minute, then take it off and wait 20 to 30 seconds. Pour in the egg and cheese, toss hard until creamy, and return the guanciale last.

    note: Carbonara sets as it cools, so serve straight away into warm bowls with a last grind of pepper, and eat at once. Don't keep leftovers, because the egg is only lightly cooked.

## About the dish

Carbonara is a Roman dish, though a fairly young one: it isn't recorded before the middle of the 20th century, and its origin is still argued over, from charcoal burners (*carbonari*) to Allied soldiers' bacon and eggs. When it's right, each strand is coated in a smooth, golden, glossy sauce with no scrambled bits, the guanciale is crisp at the edges and tender inside, and the pepper is clear. Romans leave out cream, garlic and onion. Cooks still disagree about yolks only or some whole egg, pecorino alone or mixed with Parmigiano, and spaghetti or rigatoni.

## What you need

- 400 g dried spaghetti or rigatoni
- 150 g guanciale (or pancetta) in a piece
- 4 egg yolks and 1 whole egg, very fresh (pasteurised if serving anyone vulnerable)
- 80 g Pecorino Romano, or 55 g Pecorino Romano and 25 g Parmigiano Reggiano
- Plenty of freshly cracked black pepper
- About 25 g salt for the pasta water
- Equipment: a large pot, a wide frying pan, tongs, a mixing bowl, a fine rasp grater, and a thermometer and heatproof bowl if using the water-bath method

## Where it usually goes wrong

- **Scrambled egg.** The pan was still on the heat or too hot. Take it off and wait before the eggs go in (see [Emulsifying cheese and egg sauces](../../patterns/emulsifying-cheese-and-egg-sauces/)).
- **Chewy, greasy or burnt guanciale.** It went into a hot pan or cooked past golden (see [Rendering guanciale and pancetta](../../patterns/rendering-guanciale/)).
- **Thick, clumpy sauce by the time it reaches the table.** Too little water or too slow to serve. Keep it slightly loose (see [Saving the pasta water](../../patterns/saving-pasta-water/)).
- **Salty.** Full-strength pasta water on top of guanciale and pecorino (see [Salting the pasta water](../../patterns/salting-pasta-water/)).
