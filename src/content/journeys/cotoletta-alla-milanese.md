## About the dish

The cotoletta is one of Milan's signature dishes: a thick veal rib chop, still on the bone, dipped in egg and fine breadcrumbs and fried in butter. When it's right, the crust is evenly golden, crisp and firmly attached, and the meat inside is juicy and pale pink or just cooked. Milan and Vienna have long argued about whether the cotoletta or the Wiener Schnitzel came first, and nobody has settled it; the schnitzel is boneless, thinner and floured before the egg. There are disputes in Milan too: purists skip the flour, and some frown on lemon. A popular modern version, the *orecchia d'elefante* (elephant's ear), is beaten very thin and wide and often served topped with rocket and chopped tomato.

## What you need

- 4 veal rib chops on the bone, each about 2.5 cm thick before beating (250 to 300 g each)
- 2 eggs, beaten with a pinch of salt
- About 150 g fine dry breadcrumbs from stale white bread
- About 150 g clarified butter (ghee), or 200 g butter clarified at home
- Salt
- 1 lemon, cut into wedges
- 150 g rocket or mixed salad leaves
- Extra virgin olive oil and wine vinegar for the salad
- Equipment: a meat mallet, baking paper, two wide plates, a large heavy frying pan, a probe thermometer and a wire rack

## Where it usually goes wrong

- **The crust slides off.** The meat was wet, the crumbs weren't pressed on, or the chop went straight from crumbs to pan. Press firmly and rest it, as in [Pounding and breading cutlets](../../patterns/pounding-and-breading-cutlets/).
- **Dark, bitter spots on the crust.** Whole butter's milk solids burnt. Use clarified butter and moderate the heat, as in [Pan-frying thin cutlets](../../patterns/pan-frying-thin-cutlets/).
- **Dry, grey meat under a perfect crust.** It cooked too long. Probe it and lift it out early, as in [Cooking meat by temperature](../../patterns/cooking-meat-by-temperature/).
- **A soggy underside.** It drained on paper or waited on a plate. Drain on a rack and serve straight away.
