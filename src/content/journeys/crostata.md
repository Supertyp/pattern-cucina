## About the dish

The crostata is one of the most common Italian home desserts, baked by grandparents and sold in every *pasticceria* and bakery. It's not tied to a single region, and its pastry recipe changes from family to family. When it's right, the pastry is golden, crumbly and short, crisp underneath, and the jam is glossy and set, showing through a neat lattice. Cooks disagree about baking powder in the pastry: some like the softer, cake-like crumb it gives, others leave it out for a crisper base. Both are traditional.

## What you need

- 300 g plain flour (Italian 00 or local plain flour)
- 200 g cold unsalted butter, cubed
- 100 g sugar (icing sugar for a finer crumb)
- 1 egg
- A pinch of salt and the finely grated zest of 1 lemon
- About 350 g thick jam
- Equipment: digital scale, 24 cm tart tin with a removable base, rolling pin, baking paper

## Where it usually goes wrong

- **Tough, hard pastry.** The dough was kneaded after the egg went in. Stop as soon as it holds together: see [Making pasta frolla](../../patterns/making-pasta-frolla/).
- **It cracks or smears when you roll it.** It's too cold and crumbly, or too warm and greasy. Rest it, roll between baking paper and chill again if it softens: see [Making pasta frolla](../../patterns/making-pasta-frolla/).
- **Jam boils over or runs out of the slice.** Runny jam, or too much of it. Pick a thick jam and weigh it: see [Measuring by weight](../../patterns/measuring-by-weight/).
- **It breaks as you lift it out.** Warm frolla is fragile. Let it cool fully in the tin before you unmould and slice.
