## About the dish

Fritto misto di mare is cooked on every stretch of the Italian coast, from Liguria and Venice to Naples and Sicily, and each place fries what its own boats bring in. When it's right, the coating is thin, crisp and dry, not a thick batter, the squid is tender rather than rubbery, the little fish crunch whole, bones and all, and nothing tastes of oil. Cooks disagree about the coating: plain flour, semolina or a mix, and some dip in milk first. Purists serve only lemon and salt, never a dipping sauce.

## What you need

- 300 g squid, cleaned
- 300 g raw prawns
- 300 g small whole fish (whitebait, small sardines or anchovies)
- 75 g 00 or plain flour and 75 g fine semolina
- About 2 litres peanut, sunflower or regular olive oil
- Salt flakes and 2 lemons, in wedges
- Equipment: deep heavy saucepan or electric fryer, probe or clip-on thermometer, spider, wire rack, lid or fire blanket

## Where it usually goes wrong

- **Greasy, pale seafood.** The oil dropped below 170 °C because the batches were too big. Fry a handful at a time and wait for 180 °C: see [Deep-frying at home](../../patterns/deep-frying-at-home/).
- **Rubbery squid.** It stayed in too long. Lift it out as soon as it's pale gold: see [Cooking squid and prawns](../../patterns/cooking-squid-and-prawns/).
- **Soggy, gluey coating.** The seafood was wet or floured too early. Dry it well and flour each batch at the last moment: see [Prepare before you cook](../../patterns/mise-en-place/).
- **Spitting oil.** Water on the seafood. Pat it dry and lower pieces in away from you: see [Deep-frying at home](../../patterns/deep-frying-at-home/).
