  - pattern: slow-cooked-ragu
    note: Make the Bolognese-style ragù with about 600 g mince, the day before if you can. Leave it a little looser than you would for tagliatelle, spoonable rather than stiff, because the pasta drinks liquid in the oven.
  - pattern: making-besciamella
    note: Make 1 litre of besciamella with 70 to 80 g each of butter and flour, the looser lasagne ratio. It should pour slowly from the ladle; loosen it with warm milk if it has stiffened while you roll the pasta.
  - pattern: egg-pasta-dough
    note: Use 300 g 00 flour and 3 large eggs. For the green sheets traditional in Bologna, work in about 50 g of cooked, very well squeezed and finely chopped spinach with the eggs, and allow a little extra flour.
  - pattern: rolling-pasta-sheets
    note: Roll to about 1 mm and cut the sheets to fit your dish. Thin sheets mean more layers and a lighter lasagne. Lay them on semolina-dusted cloths without overlapping.
  - pattern: assembling-baked-pasta
    note: Blanch the fresh sheets for 30 seconds, or use them raw. Build five or six layers of pasta, a thin coat of ragù, a little besciamella and Parmigiano, ending with besciamella and cheese. Bake at 180 °C (160 °C fan-forced) for 35 to 45 minutes and rest it for 20.
  - pattern: grating-hard-cheese
    note: You need about 120 g Parmigiano Reggiano. Grate it on the small holes of a box grater so it scatters evenly between layers; a good pre-grated pack is acceptable here.
  - pattern: timing-a-multi-course-meal
    note: Make the ragù a day ahead, then the besciamella and pasta in the morning. Assembled lasagne can wait covered in the fridge for up to a day once the sauces have cooled; allow an extra 15 to 20 minutes in the oven and check the centre is steaming hot.
  - pattern: wine-with-tomato-dishes
    note: Lasagne is rich with meat, cheese and besciamella, so it can take a fuller red, but keep acidity first. A Sangiovese from Romagna or Tuscany or a Barbera works well, and a dry Lambrusco is the local choice.

## About the dish

Lasagne alla bolognese is the baked feast-day pasta of Bologna and Emilia, made for Sundays, Christmas and family gatherings. When it's right, it has many thin layers that hold together on the plate, each one moist with ragù and besciamella, with a golden, slightly crisp top and chewy browned corners that everyone fights over. In Bologna the sheets are traditionally green, coloured with spinach, though plain egg pasta is common too. Outside Emilia many families use mozzarella or ricotta, but the Bolognese version keeps to ragù, besciamella and Parmigiano.

## What you need

- About 1 litre of Bolognese ragù (made with 600 g mince; see [Fresh tagliatelle with ragù](../tagliatelle-al-ragu/) for the full list, scaled up)
- 1 litre full-cream milk, 70 to 80 g butter and 70 to 80 g plain flour
- Nutmeg and salt
- 300 g 00 flour and 3 large eggs, plus 50 g cooked spinach for green sheets (optional)
- About 120 g Parmigiano Reggiano
- Equipment: baking dish about 30 x 20 cm and 6 to 8 cm deep, pasta machine or long rolling pin, whisk, foil

## Where it usually goes wrong

- **Dry, stiff lasagne.** The sauces were too thick, or the sheets were left bare. Keep the ragù and besciamella loose and cover every sheet: see [Assembling baked pasta](../../patterns/assembling-baked-pasta/).
- **Lumpy besciamella.** The milk went in all at once. Add it a splash at a time, whisking: see [Making besciamella](../../patterns/making-besciamella/).
- **It slides apart when you cut it.** It was cut straight from the oven. Rest it 15 to 20 minutes: see [Assembling baked pasta](../../patterns/assembling-baked-pasta/).
- **Thick, doughy layers.** The sheets were rolled too thick. Go to about 1 mm: see [Rolling pasta sheets](../../patterns/rolling-pasta-sheets/).
