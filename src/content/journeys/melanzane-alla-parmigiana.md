  - pattern: shopping-by-season
    note: Parmigiana is a summer dish. Make it when eggplants are firm, glossy and cheap, and basil is plentiful. In high summer you can make the sauce from ripe fresh tomatoes; the rest of the year good tins are better.
  - pattern: sharp-knife-and-stable-board
    note: Cut a thin strip off one long side of each eggplant so it lies flat, then slice it lengthwise into even 1 cm slices. Long slices are the Neapolitan way and make neat layers; rounds work too.
  - pattern: preparing-eggplant
    note: Salt the 1.5 kg of slices in a colander under a weight for 30 to 60 minutes, then pat them completely dry. Fry until deep golden and soft right through, with no firm, pale centre. Undercooked eggplant turns spongy and squeaky in the finished dish.
  - pattern: deep-frying-at-home
    note: Fry three or four slices at a time in a wide, deep pan with 1 to 2 cm of oil at 170 to 180 °C, and drain on paper towels. For a lighter version, brush the slices with oil and bake them at 200 °C instead.
  - pattern: quick-tomato-sauce
    note: Make the sauce with three 400 g tins of whole peeled tomatoes, half an onion or two garlic cloves, and 4 tablespoons of olive oil. Simmer it for the full 30 minutes, until thick, because the fried eggplant releases liquid rather than absorbing it.
  - pattern: buying-fresh-cheeses
    note: Use about 400 g fior di latte, cut into small cubes and drained in a sieve in the fridge for at least an hour, or longer. Wet mozzarella is the main cause of a watery parmigiana. Some southern cooks use smoked provola or caciocavallo instead.
  - pattern: grating-hard-cheese
    note: Grate about 100 g Parmigiano Reggiano on the small holes of a box grater, so it scatters evenly between the layers. A good pre-grated Parmigiano Reggiano is an acceptable shortcut here.
  - pattern: keeping-fresh-herbs
    note: You need a generous bunch of basil, a few torn leaves in every layer. Keep it in a glass of water on the bench, not in the fridge, and tear the leaves only as you build the dish.
  - pattern: assembling-baked-pasta
    note: Unlike pasta, eggplant doesn't drink the sauce, so keep it thick. Build three or four layers of eggplant, sauce, mozzarella, Parmigiano and basil on a thin base of sauce, ending with sauce and Parmigiano. Bake at 190 °C (170 °C fan-forced) for 35 to 40 minutes and rest 30 minutes.
  - pattern: wine-with-tomato-dishes
    note: Tomato, cheese and fried eggplant need a red with good acidity and gentle tannin. Try a Lacryma Christi del Vesuvio rosso from Campania or a Cerasuolo di Vittoria from Sicily, served slightly cool.

## About the dish

*Parmigiana di melanzane* is a dish of southern Italy, claimed by both Campania and Sicily, and the name is disputed too: some link it to Parmigiano, others to a Sicilian word for the slats of a shutter, which the overlapping slices resemble. When it's right, it cuts into neat, moist slices: soft, silky eggplant, a thick, sweet tomato sauce and melted cheese, without a pool of water or oil on the plate. Many people prefer it warm rather than hot, or the next day. Versions vary: some dip the slices in flour or egg before frying, some Sicilian cooks use caciocavallo or pecorino, and baked rather than fried eggplant is common in Italian homes too.

## What you need

- 3 large eggplants (about 1.5 kg)
- Fine salt
- About 750 ml sunflower, peanut or olive oil for frying
- 3 × 400 g tins whole peeled tomatoes
- Half an onion or 2 garlic cloves
- 4 tablespoons extra virgin olive oil for the sauce
- 400 g fior di latte mozzarella
- 100 g Parmigiano Reggiano
- A large bunch of basil
- Equipment: a colander and weight, a wide deep frying pan, a thermometer, a 20 by 30 cm ovenproof dish about 6 cm deep, and a lid or fire blanket nearby

## Where it usually goes wrong

- **Heavy and greasy.** The oil was too cool or the slices were wet, so they soaked up oil. Dry them and keep the temperature up, as in [Deep-frying at home](../../patterns/deep-frying-at-home/) and [Preparing eggplant](../../patterns/preparing-eggplant/).
- **Watery, sloppy layers.** Undrained mozzarella or a thin sauce. Drain the cheese, as in [Buying fresh cheeses](../../patterns/buying-fresh-cheeses/), and simmer the [sauce](../../patterns/quick-tomato-sauce/) until thick.
- **It collapses when you cut it.** It was cut straight from the oven. Rest it at least 30 minutes, as in [Assembling baked pasta](../../patterns/assembling-baked-pasta/).
- **Spongy, squeaky eggplant in the layers.** The slices came out of the oil before they were soft right through. Fry until creamy inside, as in [Preparing eggplant](../../patterns/preparing-eggplant/).
