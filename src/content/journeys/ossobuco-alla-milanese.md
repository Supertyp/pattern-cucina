    note: Snip the membrane round each shank in two or three places and tie a string round it, so it stays flat and keeps its shape. Dust lightly in flour and brown both sides in butter and a little oil, two shanks at a time.
  - pattern: dicing-soffritto-vegetables

  - pattern: building-a-soffritto

    note: For the gremolata, mince 1 small clove very fine, germ removed since it's eaten raw, with the zest of a lemon and a handful of flat-leaf parsley. Make it in the last 10 minutes so it stays bright and sharp.

    note: The braise can be made a day ahead and gently reheated until piping hot, which leaves only the polenta and gremolata for the day. Scatter the gremolata over each shank at the table so its aroma hits the warm sauce.

    note: This is a full dish with rich marrow and a bright lemon finish, so pick a medium to full red with good acidity. A Nebbiolo from Valtellina, Lombardy's own, or a Barbera both work.

## About the dish

*Ossobuco* means "bone with a hole", and the marrow inside the bone is the prize: in Milan it's scooped out with a small spoon. When it's right, the meat is soft enough to cut with a fork but still holds together on the bone, the sauce is glossy and lightly thickened by the flour and gelatine, and the gremolata lifts it with fresh lemon and garlic. The oldest Milanese versions are *in bianco*, with no tomato, and many cooks still make it that way, while others add a little tomato. In Milan it's classically served with saffron risotto (see [Saffron risotto](../risotto-alla-milanese/)); polenta, as here, is a common and equally traditional northern partner.

## What you need

- 4 veal shanks cut 3 to 4 cm thick (about 1.2 kg)
- Plain flour, for dusting
- 50 g butter and 2 tablespoons olive oil
- About 300 g onion, carrot and celery
- 150 ml dry white wine
- About 500 ml hot light meat stock
- Gremolata: 1 lemon, 1 small garlic clove, a handful of flat-leaf parsley
- 250 g coarse polenta (*bramata*), 1 litre water, 10 g salt
- 30 g butter and 40 g Parmigiano Reggiano for the polenta
- Equipment: heavy lidded pot that fits the shanks in one layer, kitchen string, wooden spoon

## Where it usually goes wrong

- **The shanks curl up and the marrow falls out.** The membrane wasn't snipped, the shanks weren't tied, or they were turned roughly. Prepare them before browning and baste rather than flip: see [Browning meat](../../patterns/browning-meat/).
- **Stringy, dry meat.** The braise boiled hard. Keep it at a tremble and test with a fork, not the clock: see [Braising meat](../../patterns/braising-meat/).
- **Gritty polenta.** It was taken off after 15 minutes because it looked thick. Give coarse meal the full 40 to 50 minutes: see [Cooking polenta](../../patterns/cooking-polenta/).
- **Harsh, raw-tasting gremolata.** Old garlic with its green germ, or too much of it. Use one small clove, chopped very fine: see [Preparing garlic](../../patterns/preparing-garlic/).
