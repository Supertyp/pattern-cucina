  - pattern: cooking-dried-legumes
    note: Soak 250 g of dried borlotti overnight, then simmer gently with a sage sprig and a crushed garlic clove for 1 to 2 hours, until creamy. Keep every drop of the cooking liquid, because it becomes the broth.
  - pattern: dicing-soffritto-vegetables
    note: Dice one onion, a carrot and a celery stalk to about 5 mm. Part of the soup gets mashed, so the vegetables don't need to keep their shape as they would in a minestrone.
  - pattern: rendering-guanciale
    note: Cut 80 g of pancetta (or guanciale) into small cubes and render it slowly in the soup pot. Unlike in a pasta sauce, leave the pieces in, since the soffritto cooks in their fat.
  - pattern: building-a-soffritto
    note: Add the vegetables to the pork fat with a spoonful of olive oil and cook gently for 10 to 15 minutes. Stir in a rosemary sprig and a tablespoon of tomato paste for the last minute, then add the beans and their liquid.
  - pattern: matching-pasta-shape-to-sauce
    note: Use a small soup shape such as ditalini, or a mix of broken short pasta (pasta mista) as in Naples. In the Veneto, fresh egg pasta strips are common instead.
  - pattern: portioning-pasta
    note: Use about 200 g of pasta for four to six, a little more than for a broth, because this is a thick soup. Cook it in the soup, adding hot water or bean liquid as it absorbs, and stir often so it doesn't stick.
  - pattern: seasoning-and-tasting
    note: Mash or blend a third of the beans to thicken the soup before the pasta goes in. The pancetta and a Parmigiano rind (if you add one) bring salt, so taste only once the pasta is cooked.
  - pattern: choosing-extra-virgin-olive-oil
    note: Finish each bowl with a thread of raw, robust, grassy oil and some black pepper. Let the soup rest for 5 to 10 minutes before serving, as many Italians eat it warm rather than scalding.

## About the dish

Pasta e fagioli is a peasant dish made all over Italy, with as many versions as regions. In the Veneto it's made with borlotti beans and often egg pasta; in Naples with mixed short pasta cooked until the soup is thick enough to hold a spoon. When it's right, the soup is creamy and dense, somewhere between a soup and a pasta dish, the beans are soft right through and the pasta is just tender. Cooks disagree about tomato or none, pancetta or a vegetarian base, and how thick it should be; the version here uses a little of each tradition.

## What you need

- 250 g dried borlotti beans (or two 400 g tins, drained and rinsed)
- 80 g pancetta or guanciale, in a piece
- 1 onion (about 150 g), 1 carrot and 1 celery stalk (about 75 g each)
- 2 garlic cloves, a sprig of sage and a sprig of rosemary
- 1 tablespoon tomato paste
- A piece of Parmigiano Reggiano rind (optional)
- 200 g ditalini or mixed short pasta
- Extra virgin olive oil, salt and black pepper
- Equipment: a large bowl for soaking, a heavy soup pot and a potato masher or stick blender

## Where it usually goes wrong

- **Beans still hard after hours.** Old beans, or tomato added too early. Cook the beans fully before the tomato goes in (see [Cooking dried beans, chickpeas and lentils](../../patterns/cooking-dried-legumes/)).
- **Pasta stuck to the bottom of the pot.** The soup was too thick when the pasta went in. Loosen it with hot liquid and stir (see [Portioning pasta](../../patterns/portioning-pasta/)).
- **Too salty.** Salted as usual on top of the pancetta and rind (see [Seasoning and tasting as you go](../../patterns/seasoning-and-tasting/)).
