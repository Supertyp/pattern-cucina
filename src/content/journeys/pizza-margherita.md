  - pattern: choosing-flour
    note: This dough rests two days in the fridge, so use a strong flour of about 12 to 13 % protein, ideally an Italian pizza 00 that lists W 250 to 320. A local bread flour works well too.
  - pattern: measuring-by-weight
    note: Weigh everything, water included. The yeast is only about 1 g, so use a scale that reads to 0.1 g, or dissolve it in 100 g of the recipe's water.
  - pattern: dough-hydration
    note: Use 62 % hydration, which is 372 g water and 17 g salt for 600 g flour. That sits inside the Neapolitan range and still holds its shape on the peel; drop to 60 % for your first batch if handling worries you.
  - pattern: using-yeast-and-preferments
    note: For a 48-hour cold rest you need very little, about 1 g instant dried yeast (or 3 g fresh), mixed into cool water. Add the salt once the flour is mostly mixed in, then knead until smooth.
  - pattern: cold-fermenting-dough
    note: Leave the dough an hour at room temperature, then refrigerate it for about 48 hours. For pizza on Saturday night, mix on Thursday evening.
  - pattern: balling-and-proofing-dough
    note: Divide into four balls of about 245 g, either the night before or the morning of baking. Take them out of the fridge 3 to 4 hours before you bake, and wait for the slow, partial spring-back of the poke test.
  - pattern: choosing-tomatoes
    note: The tomato goes on raw and is the star, so use San Marzano DOP or your best tin of whole peeled tomatoes. Crush by hand, drain off the thin juice and season with salt only.
  - pattern: buying-fresh-cheeses
    note: Use fior di latte, the classic margherita cheese, about 90 g per pizza, torn small and drained in a sieve in the fridge for an hour or more. Buffalo mozzarella makes a wetter pizza, often sold as a *bufalina*.
  - pattern: stretching-pizza-by-hand
    note: Open each ball to about 28 to 30 cm, with a centre around 3 mm thick and a puffy rim of 1 to 2 cm. Stretch one at a time, right before topping, and keep the others covered.
  - pattern: topping-pizza-with-restraint
    note: On the peel, spread 70 to 80 g of tomato in a spiral, scatter the drained mozzarella with gaps between pieces, and launch within a minute. In a home oven add the basil after baking, or tuck a few leaves under the cheese so they don't scorch.
  - pattern: baking-pizza-at-home
    note: Preheat the steel or stone at maximum for 45 to 60 minutes, grill on for the last 10. Bake one pizza at a time for 5 to 8 minutes, turning once, with 5 minutes' recovery between. Eat each as it comes out.
  - pattern: choosing-extra-virgin-olive-oil
    note: Finish each hot pizza with the basil and a thin spiral of your best fresh extra virgin oil, about a teaspoon. The raw oil's fruitiness is part of the flavour, so a stale bottle shows.

## About the dish

The margherita is Naples' best-known pizza: a soft, thin centre with tomato, mozzarella, basil and olive oil, and a puffy, spotted rim. The famous story says it was made in 1889 for Queen Margherita in the colours of the Italian flag, but similar pizzas existed earlier, so treat it as a good story. *Pizza napoletana* is protected in the EU as a traditional speciality (STG). A wood-fired oven bakes it at over 400 °C in about 90 seconds; a home oven can't match that, so this version is a little crisper and drier, but still light and full of flavour.

## What you need

- 600 g strong flour (12 to 13 % protein)
- 372 g cool water
- 17 g fine salt, plus a pinch for the tomato
- 1 g instant dried yeast (or 3 g fresh yeast)
- 400 g tin whole peeled tomatoes
- 360 g fior di latte mozzarella
- A small bunch of basil
- Extra virgin olive oil to finish
- Flour or fine semolina for dusting
- Equipment: a scale reading to 0.1 g, a lidded container, a pizza steel or stone, a peel and thick oven gloves

## Where it usually goes wrong

- **A soggy centre.** Too much tomato or undrained mozzarella. Follow [Topping pizza with restraint](../../patterns/topping-pizza-with-restraint/).
- **A flat, dense rim.** The edge was pressed or rolled. Leave the border alone, as in [Stretching pizza by hand](../../patterns/stretching-pizza-by-hand/).
- **A pale base under a done top.** The steel or stone wasn't heated through. Preheat for the full time, as in [Baking pizza in a home oven](../../patterns/baking-pizza-at-home/).
