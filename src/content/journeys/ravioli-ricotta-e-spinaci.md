  - pattern: buying-fresh-cheeses
    note: Buy 250 g of fresh basket ricotta and drain it overnight in the fridge until it holds a shape. The filling only heats briefly in the pot, so don't rely on it reaching 75 °C; anyone pregnant or at higher risk of *Listeria* should give this one a miss.
  - pattern: blanching-and-shocking-vegetables
    note: Blanch 300 g of spinach leaves for about 30 seconds, shock them in iced water, then squeeze them in a tea towel until no more water runs out. You'll have a tight ball of about 150 g; chop it fine.
  - pattern: egg-pasta-dough
    note: Use 300 g 00 flour and 3 large eggs. Aim for the firm end of the range, because a soft dough tears when rolled as thin as filled pasta needs.
  - pattern: kneading-and-resting-dough
    note: While the dough rests for 30 minutes, mix the ricotta, spinach, 50 g Parmigiano, a pinch of nutmeg and salt. Taste it, then add 1 egg yolk and keep it in the fridge.
  - pattern: rolling-pasta-sheets
    note: Roll one egg-sized piece at a time to the thinnest setting, about 0.5 to 0.8 mm, so you can see your hand through it. Keep the rest wrapped, and fill each sheet the moment it's rolled.
  - pattern: shaping-filled-pasta
    note: Place teaspoons of cold filling about 3 cm apart along half a strip, fold, and press the air out from the fold towards the edges before cutting into squares of 5 to 6 cm. Expect 40 to 50 ravioli.
  - pattern: drying-and-storing-fresh-pasta
    note: Never air-dry ravioli; the filling makes them spoil from inside. Hold them in one layer on a semolina-dusted tray for an hour or two, or open-freeze them straight after shaping and cook from frozen.
  - pattern: salting-pasta-water
    note: Use the full 10 g per litre, since butter and sage add little salt. A wide pot of 4 litres lets you cook in two batches without crowding.
  - pattern: finishing-pasta-in-the-pan
    note: Ravioli are the exception here. Melt 80 g butter with a dozen sage leaves until it foams and the leaves crisp, then lift the cooked ravioli in with a slotted spoon, add a spoonful of pasta water and swirl the pan gently rather than tossing.
  - pattern: grating-hard-cheese
    note: Use the small holes of a box grater for the 50 g in the filling, weighed, and a rasp for the fluffy Parmigiano scattered over at the table.

## About the dish

Ravioli filled with ricotta and greens are made across northern and central Italy, from Emilia's *tortelli d'erbetta* to Tuscan versions, often for feast days, and every area has its own shape, green and name. When they're right, the pasta is thin and tender with no thick, chewy edges, the filling is soft, creamy and well seasoned, and each one comes out of the pot whole. Spinach is the common choice outside Italy; many Italian cooks prefer chard (*bietole*) or wild greens, and some leave out the egg yolk entirely. All are traditional somewhere.

## What you need

- 300 g 00 flour, plus fine semolina for dusting
- 3 large eggs, plus 1 yolk for the filling
- 250 g fresh ricotta, drained
- 300 g spinach (or silverbeet) leaves
- 50 g Parmigiano Reggiano for the filling, plus more to serve
- Nutmeg, salt and pepper
- 80 g butter and about 12 sage leaves
- Equipment: pasta machine or long rolling pin, fluted pastry wheel or knife, slotted spoon, clean tea towel, sieve

## Where it usually goes wrong

- **Ravioli burst in the pot.** Wet filling, trapped air or a hard boil. Drain and squeeze thoroughly, press the air out and keep the water at a lively simmer: see [Shaping and sealing filled pasta](../../patterns/shaping-filled-pasta/).
- **The seams won't stick.** The sheet dried before you filled it. Roll one piece at a time and fill at once: see [Rolling pasta sheets](../../patterns/rolling-pasta-sheets/).
- **Thick, chewy edges.** The sheet wasn't thin enough for a double layer. Go to the thinnest setting: see [Rolling pasta sheets](../../patterns/rolling-pasta-sheets/).
- **Bland filling.** Ricotta is mild, and once the raw yolk is in you can't taste it. Season and taste first: see [Shaping and sealing filled pasta](../../patterns/shaping-filled-pasta/).
