    note: A light meat brodo (beef and chicken, or chicken alone) is the Milanese choice, and you need about 1.3 litres. Saffron is delicate, so if you use bought stock, dilute it half and half with water. Ask the butcher for a marrow bone too, if you want the traditional marrow.

    note: Use 320 g of **Carnaroli** for four first-course plates. Its firm grain holds up to the full 18 minutes and keeps a clear bite inside the creamy saffron sauce. Don't rinse it.
  - pattern: mise-en-place
    note: Before the onion goes in, crumble the saffron threads into a cup with a ladle of hot brodo and leave them to steep for at least 15 minutes. Dice the cold butter, grate the cheese and set the brodo at a bare simmer beside the risotto pan.
  - pattern: dicing-soffritto-vegetables
    note: Only onion here, no carrot or celery. Dice one small onion (about 80 g) very finely, 2 to 3 mm, so it melts away completely, then soften it in 30 g butter (with about 30 g diced bone marrow, if using) over low heat for 6 to 8 minutes, without colour.
  - pattern: toasting-rice-for-risotto
    note: Toast the rice for 2 to 3 minutes until the edges turn translucent. Keep it from browning, because the dish's colour should come from the saffron alone and toasted notes would cover its honeyed aroma.
  - pattern: cooking-with-wine
    note: Add about 100 ml of dry, unoaked white and stir until the sharp smell has gone. Some Milanese cooks leave the wine out entirely for a softer, rounder risotto, which works just as well; add a first ladle of brodo instead.
  - pattern: adding-stock-to-risotto
    note: Stir in the saffron and its golden liquid about 12 minutes in, so the colour spreads evenly but the aroma isn't simmered away. Start tasting at 14 minutes and stop while it still flows *all'onda*.
  - pattern: finishing-risotto
    note: Off the heat, beat in 40 g cold diced butter and 60 g finely grated Grana Padano or Parmigiano Reggiano, then rest it covered for 1 to 2 minutes. It should be a deep, even yellow, glossy and flowing. Taste for salt only now.
  - pattern: pairing-by-region
    note: Lombardy's own wines suit this rich, buttery risotto. Try a Franciacorta (the region's traditional-method sparkling wine) or a Lugana white from the southern shore of Lake Garda, both with the acidity to cut through the butter (see [matching weight](../../patterns/matching-wine-weight-to-food/)).

## About the dish

Risotto alla milanese is the yellow risotto of Milan, made with saffron, butter, onion, brodo and grated cheese. A popular legend credits a glazier's apprentice at Milan's cathedral in the 16th century, who is said to have coloured a wedding risotto with the saffron used for stained glass, but there's no firm evidence for it. Done right, it's a warm golden yellow all through, creamy and loose enough to spread on the plate, with grains that still have a little bite and a gentle, honeyed saffron scent. It's served as a primo, and also as the classic partner of ossobuco. Cooks disagree about the details: whether beef marrow belongs (it's traditional, but many leave it out), and whether to use white wine or none at all.

## What you need

- 320 g Carnaroli rice
- About 1.3 litres light meat or chicken brodo, plus a little hot water in reserve
- About 0.2 g saffron threads (a generous pinch), or two 0.1 g sachets of powdered saffron
- 1 small onion (about 80 g)
- 70 g cold butter (30 g to soften the onion, 40 g to finish)
- 30 g beef bone marrow (optional)
- 100 ml dry white wine (optional)
- 60 g Grana Padano or Parmigiano Reggiano, in a piece
- Salt
- Equipment: a heavy, wide pan or risotto pan, a second pot for the brodo, a ladle and a wooden spoon

## Where it usually goes wrong

- **Pale, patchy colour or a bitter, medicinal taste.** Dry threads tossed straight in don't release their colour evenly, and too much saffron turns bitter. Steep a modest amount ahead, as in [Prepare before you cook](../../patterns/mise-en-place/).
- **Stiff and gluey on the plate.** The risotto was cooked until it looked finished in the pan, or it waited too long. Stop while it's loose, as in [Adding stock to risotto](../../patterns/adding-stock-to-risotto/), and serve straight after [the mantecatura](../../patterns/finishing-risotto/).
- **Too salty.** Bought stock reduces and the cheese adds more salt. Use light, unsalted [brodo](../../patterns/making-brodo/) and season only after the cheese goes in.
