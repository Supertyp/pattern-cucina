## About the dish

*Saltimbocca* means "jumps into the mouth", which says how quick and tasty it is. In the Roman version each thin slice of veal is topped with a sage leaf and a slice of prosciutto, pinned with a toothpick, cooked in butter and finished with white wine. When it's right, the veal is tender and juicy, the prosciutto lightly crisp at the edges, the sage fragrant, and there's just a few spoonfuls of glossy, savoury sauce. Cooks disagree on the details: whether to flour the meat, whether to cook the prosciutto side first, and whether to roll the slices up, which is done in some places but not in the classic flat Roman version. Marsala sometimes replaces the white wine for a sweeter sauce.

## What you need

- 8 thin veal escalopes (about 560 g in all)
- 8 thin slices prosciutto crudo (about 100 g)
- 8 large sage leaves
- Plain flour for dusting
- 20 g butter and 1 tablespoon olive oil for frying
- 100 ml dry white wine
- 40 g cold butter, diced, to finish the sauce
- About 800 g spinach or 1 kg chicory
- 2 garlic cloves, a pinch of dried chilli and 3 tablespoons extra virgin olive oil for the greens
- Salt and pepper
- Equipment: toothpicks, a meat mallet, a wide frying pan and a second pan for the greens

## Where it usually goes wrong

- **Tough, dry veal.** The slices were too thick, the pan too crowded or they cooked too long. Pound them thin and cook in batches, as in [Pan-frying thin cutlets](../../patterns/pan-frying-thin-cutlets/).
- **Too salty.** The meat was salted on top of salty prosciutto. Taste the finished sauce first, as in [Buying cured meats](../../patterns/buying-cured-meats/).
- **A greasy, split sauce.** The butter went into a boiling pan. Take it off the heat first, as in [Cooking with wine](../../patterns/cooking-with-wine/) and [Pan-frying thin cutlets](../../patterns/pan-frying-thin-cutlets/).
- **Cold meat while the greens finish.** Everything wasn't ready first. Follow [Prepare before you cook](../../patterns/mise-en-place/).
