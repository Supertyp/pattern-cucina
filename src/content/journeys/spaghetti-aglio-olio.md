    note: This is the dish the pantry is built for. Spaghetti, extra virgin olive oil, garlic, dried chilli and salt are all long-keeping, and the only fresh thing is a small bunch of flat-leaf parsley, which you can leave out at a pinch.
  - pattern: choosing-dried-pasta
    note: With nothing but oil to hold on to, the pasta matters more here than in almost any other dish. Choose a pale, matte, bronze-die spaghetti, and expect it to take a minute or two longer than the packet says.
  - pattern: choosing-extra-virgin-olive-oil
    note: The oil is the sauce, so use a fresh, sound extra virgin for the cooking and, if you have one, keep a little of your better finishing oil for a few drops over the bowls off the heat.
  - pattern: mise-en-place
    note: The whole sauce takes about as long as the pasta, so put the water on first, then slice the garlic, crumble the chilli, chop the parsley and set a mug for pasta water beside the stove. Warm two bowls.
  - pattern: preparing-garlic
    note: Use 2 or 3 cloves, sliced thinly and evenly, about 1 to 2 mm, so they all turn gold together. Flick out any green germ, since the garlic is the main flavour and there's nothing to hide harshness.
  - pattern: salting-pasta-water
    note: Use 2 litres of water and the full 20 g of salt. Unlike Roman cheese sauces, there's nothing salty in this sauce, so the water does almost all the seasoning.
  - pattern: cooking-pasta-al-dente
    note: Start tasting 3 minutes before the packet time and pull the spaghetti while it's still noticeably firm in the centre, because it gets another minute or two in the oil.
  - pattern: infusing-oil-with-garlic-and-chilli
    note: For 200 g of pasta, about 50 ml of oil is right. Start it in a cold wide pan when the pasta goes in, add dried chilli as the garlic just colours, and stop at pale gold with a ladle of pasta water.
  - pattern: saving-pasta-water
    note: Scoop out a full mug before you drain. Oil sauces need more water than any other, roughly 3 or 4 tablespoons per portion, and it's what turns the oil from a greasy film into a cloudy, clinging sauce.
  - pattern: finishing-pasta-in-the-pan
    note: Lift the spaghetti straight into the garlic oil and toss hard over medium to high heat for a minute or two, adding water a spoonful at a time, until it looks glossy and slightly loose. Stir in the parsley off the heat.
  - pattern: cheese-on-pasta
    note: Many cooks serve it without cheese and some add a little pecorino. Taste it first. If you want something on top, a spoonful of crisp toasted breadcrumbs keeps the oil and garlic in charge.
  - pattern: eating-pasta
    note: An oil sauce tightens as it cools, so twirl and eat straight away from warm bowls, a few strands at a time against the side of the bowl.

## About the dish

Spaghetti aglio, olio e peperoncino belongs to Rome and Naples and to every Italian kitchen in between. It's the classic *spaghettata di mezzanotte*, the midnight spaghetti cooked for friends after a night out, because it needs nothing from the shops. When it's right, each strand is coated in a light, slightly creamy film of oil, the garlic is sweet and nutty rather than sharp, and the chilli gives a warm glow rather than a burn. Cooks argue about the details: sliced or whole garlic, parsley or none, and whether a little cheese belongs on top.

## What you need

- 200 g dried spaghetti
- About 50 ml extra virgin olive oil, plus a little to finish
- 2 or 3 garlic cloves
- 1 small dried red chilli, or a pinch of chilli flakes (or half a fresh red chilli)
- A small handful of flat-leaf parsley
- 20 g salt for the pasta water
- Equipment: a large pot, a wide frying pan (about 26 to 28 cm), tongs and a heatproof mug

## Where it usually goes wrong

- **Bitter, brown garlic.** The oil was too hot or the garlic sat too long. Start it in cold oil and stop at pale gold (see [Infusing oil with garlic and chilli](../../patterns/infusing-oil-with-garlic-and-chilli/)).
- **Greasy, slippery pasta with oil pooling in the bowl.** There wasn't enough starchy water or tossing to bring oil and water together (see [Finishing pasta in the pan](../../patterns/finishing-pasta-in-the-pan/)).
- **Bland pasta.** With such a plain sauce, under-salted water can't be fixed later (see [Salting the pasta water](../../patterns/salting-pasta-water/)).
