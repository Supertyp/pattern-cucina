  - pattern: cleaning-mussels-and-clams

  - pattern: mise-en-place
    note: Put the pasta water on while the clams finish purging. Slice 3 garlic cloves, chop a bunch of parsley, measure the wine, and set out a bowl for opened clams and a sieve lined with paper towel.
  - pattern: infusing-oil-with-garlic-and-chilli
    note: Use about 80 ml of oil, the garlic and a small dried chilli in a wide pan with a lid. Stop at pale gold, because the clams go in next and the garlic keeps cooking in their steam.
  - pattern: cooking-with-wine
    note: Use about 100 ml of a dry, unoaked white, such as a Falanghina you'll drink with the meal. If you cook on gas, take the pan off the flame as you pour it in with the clams.
  - pattern: cooking-mussels-and-clams
    note: Cover over high heat, shake the pan, and lift out clams as they open, usually after 2 to 4 minutes. Strain the liquor through the lined sieve and throw away any that stay firmly shut.
  - pattern: salting-pasta-water
    note: Use 4 litres of water and only about 6 to 7 g of salt per litre, as for a salty sauce. The clam liquor is seawater, and the pasta will soak it up.
  - pattern: cooking-pasta-al-dente
    note: Spaghetti or linguine both work. Drain about 2 minutes short of al dente, because the pasta finishes cooking in the clam juices and absorbs their flavour.
  - pattern: finishing-pasta-in-the-pan
    note: Toss the pasta in the strained liquor with a splash of fresh oil over high heat until the juices thicken and cling. You rarely need pasta water here. Add the clams and parsley for the last 30 seconds.
  - pattern: cheese-on-pasta
    note: Leave the cheese off. Grated cheese covers the sweet, briny taste of the clams. A few drops of good oil and the parsley are all it needs.
  - pattern: wine-with-seafood
    note: Serve the rest of the Falanghina, or another Campanian white such as Fiano or Greco di Tufo, well chilled. Their acidity works like a squeeze of lemon on the clams.

## About the dish

Spaghetti alle vongole is one of the great dishes of Naples and the Campanian coast, and it's eaten all along Italy's shores. The classic version is *in bianco*, without tomato, though a version with a few cherry tomatoes (*rosso*) is also popular, and cooks argue about it as well as about wine or no wine. When it's right, the spaghetti is glossy and slightly golden from the clam juices, tastes of the sea rather than of salt, and the clams are plump and tender, with no grit at all. Some cooks shell part of the clams so the dish is easier to eat.

## What you need

- 400 g dried spaghetti or linguine
- 1 kg small live clams (vongole), pipis or cockles
- About 80 ml extra virgin olive oil, plus a splash to finish
- 3 garlic cloves
- 1 small dried red chilli (or a pinch of chilli flakes)
- 100 ml dry white wine
- A bunch of flat-leaf parsley
- Salt for purging the clams and for the pasta water
- Equipment: a large bowl and colander, a wide pan with a lid (about 30 cm), tongs, a sieve and paper towel or cloth

## Where it usually goes wrong

- **Sand in the sauce.** The clams weren't purged, or the liquor wasn't strained (see [Cleaning mussels and clams](../../patterns/cleaning-mussels-and-clams/)).
- **Rubbery, shrunken clams.** They stayed in the pan after opening. Lift them out as they open (see [Cooking mussels and clams](../../patterns/cooking-mussels-and-clams/)).
- **Too salty.** The pasta water was fully salted on top of the clam liquor (see [Salting the pasta water](../../patterns/salting-pasta-water/)).
