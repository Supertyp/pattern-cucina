## About the dish

Tiramisù ("pick me up") is a young dessert, appearing in the late 1960s and 1970s, and both Treviso in the Veneto and towns in Friuli claim to have invented it, a rivalry that is still argued with some heat. When it's right, it cuts into neat, soft layers: biscuits moist but not soggy, a cream that is light, smooth and holds its shape, and a bitter dusting of cocoa on top. Cooks disagree on alcohol: many add a splash of Marsala, rum or brandy to the coffee or cream, while others insist the original had none. Both are good.

## What you need

- 500 g mascarpone, cold
- 4 large eggs (about 200 g shelled), very fresh, or pasteurised eggs
- 100 g caster sugar
- About 300 g savoiardi (sponge finger biscuits)
- 300 ml strong moka coffee, cooled
- 2 to 3 tablespoons Marsala, rum or brandy (optional)
- Unsweetened cocoa powder, for dusting
- Equipment: electric mixer, moka pot, a dish about 20 x 30 cm, fine sieve

## Where it usually goes wrong

- **The cream is grainy or split.** The mascarpone was beaten hard on high speed, or mixed into a still-warm cooked yolk base. Loosen it alone, add the yolks gradually and fold gently: see [Working with mascarpone](../../patterns/working-with-mascarpone/).
- **It slumps across the plate.** The whites were under-whipped or knocked flat by over-folding, or the biscuits were soaked rather than dipped. Get proper soft peaks and fold in thirds: see [Whipping eggs and sugar](../../patterns/whipping-eggs-and-sugar/).
- **Soggy, wet base.** Biscuits left in warm coffee collapse and leak a puddle under the cream. Cool the coffee fully and dip quickly: see [Making coffee in a moka pot](../../patterns/making-moka-pot-coffee/).
- **Served too soon.** A tiramisù made that afternoon won't slice cleanly. Plan for an overnight chill: see [Timing a multi-course meal](../../patterns/timing-a-multi-course-meal/).
