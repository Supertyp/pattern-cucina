## Summary

This pattern is for anyone baking pizza in an ordinary kitchen oven. It describes how to store up heat in a stone or steel, use the grill for top heat, launch the pizza safely, and stay safe around very hot surfaces.

| Recommendation | Why? |
| --- | --- |
| Bake on a pizza steel or stone, preheated at the oven's maximum (usually 250 to 280 °C) for 45 to 60 minutes. | A heavy, dense surface stores heat and pours it into the base on contact. The air heats up in minutes, but the stone or steel needs much longer to heat through. |
| Put the steel or stone on a shelf in the upper third, about 20 cm below the grill element, and switch the grill on for the last 10 minutes of preheating and during the bake. | The base cooks by contact from below and the top by radiant heat from above. The grill helps the top keep pace with the hot base, so the rim puffs and blisters before the base burns. |
| Launch the pizza from a lightly floured or semolina-dusted peel with a quick, confident jerk. | A thin layer of flour acts like ball bearings, and a hesitant slide lets the soft dough fold over or stick. |
| Bake for 5 to 8 minutes, turning once, then handle the steel, stone and peel only with dry, thick oven gloves. | Turning evens out hot spots. Steel and stone stay hot enough to burn for over an hour after the oven is off. |

## Context

This pattern suits Neapolitan-style and other thin, round pizzas in an electric or gas domestic oven.

It does not suit tray pizza and focaccia, which bake in a tin at a lower temperature (see [Baking focaccia in a tray](../baking-focaccia/)), or outdoor pizza ovens, which reach 400 °C or more and bake in 60 to 90 seconds.

There's a trade-off between equipment and results. A heavy baking tray, preheated, makes decent pizza for no extra cost. A stone is cheaper and more forgiving, while a steel conducts heat faster and gives the crispest, most coloured base, but it is heavy and expensive. This pattern recommends a steel or stone for anyone who bakes pizza often. Start with a preheated upside-down tray if you're not sure yet.

## Usage

**Setting up the oven:** check where your grill element is. If it's at the top of the main oven, use it, but if your only grill is at the bottom or in a separate compartment, skip that step and bake a few minutes longer. Use the conventional (non-fan) setting at maximum. Fan-forced helps top colour but can dry the pizza. Some ovens switch off the grill when the oven is already very hot, so if yours does, preheat 20 °C or so lower, then switch to the grill just before launching.

**Launching and baking:** assemble the pizza on the peel (see [Topping pizza with restraint](../topping-pizza-with-restraint/)), shake it to check it slides, set the tip of the peel at the back of the steel, and pull the peel out sharply so the pizza lands in one movement. After 3 to 4 minutes, rotate it with tongs or the peel. It's done when the rim is puffed and spotted dark brown, the cheese bubbles and the base is crisp and coloured when you lift an edge. Give the steel 5 minutes to recover between pizzas.

**Staying safe:** keep children and pets away from the open oven. Use proper oven gloves, not a damp tea towel, which conducts heat straight to your hand. Never pour water on a hot stone, as it can crack. Burnt flour and semolina smoke on the steel, so brush them off between pizzas with a dry brush and keep the kitchen ventilated. Let the stone or steel cool completely in the oven before moving it.
