## Summary

This pattern is for anyone making pizza or rolls from a batch of dough. It describes how to divide it evenly, shape tight balls, and proof them until they're airy and ready to stretch.

| Recommendation | Why? |
| --- | --- |
| Divide the dough by weight, about 230 to 270 g per pizza of 28 to 32 cm. | Equal balls bake in the same time and stretch to the same size. Traditional Neapolitan balls weigh 200 to 280 g. |
| Shape each piece into a tight, smooth ball with the seam underneath. | Tucking the dough under itself stretches the surface into a taut skin that traps gas evenly and keeps the round shape as it rises. |
| Proof the balls covered, spaced apart, until they have grown by about half and look soft and airy, usually 2 to 6 hours at room temperature. | Covering stops a dry skin forming, and the rest lets the gluten relax so the dough stretches without springing back. |
| Use the poke test: a floured fingertip dent should spring back slowly and only partly. | A dent that springs back at once means the dough is still tight, and one that stays or collapses means the gas and gluten are spent. |

## Context

This pattern suits round pizza, calzone and bread rolls, whether the dough was made the same day or cold-fermented (see [Cold-fermenting dough](../cold-fermenting-dough/)).

It does not suit focaccia and tray pizza, which proof directly in the oiled tray (see [Baking focaccia in a tray](../baking-focaccia/)), or fresh pasta dough, which rests but doesn't rise.

There's a trade-off between a firm ball and an easy stretch. A very tight ball holds its shape and rises well, but if it's shaped too tight or too late it fights back when you stretch it. A loose ball is easy to open but can spread flat and bake pale and dense. This pattern aims for firm but not torn. When in doubt, ball the dough earlier, so it has longer to relax.

## Usage

**Dividing and shaping:** turn the dough onto a lightly floured bench and cut it with a scraper rather than tearing it. Weigh each piece and add or remove small bits until they match (see [Measuring by weight](../measuring-by-weight/)). To shape, fold the edges into the centre, flip the piece seam side down, then cup your hands around it and drag it towards you on an unfloured patch of bench. The friction tightens the surface. Stop when it's smooth and domed. If the surface tears, you've gone too far: let it rest 10 minutes and finish gently. Pinch the seam closed underneath.

**Proofing:** put the balls in a lightly oiled container, on an oiled tray covered with plastic wrap, or in individual lidded tubs, with space for them to double. Cold dough from the fridge needs 2 to 4 hours at room temperature, same-day dough often less. They're ready when they've relaxed sideways, feel soft and puffy, and show small bubbles under the skin.

**Fixing over- and under-proofing:** under-proofed dough is tight, shrinks back when stretched and bakes dense. Give it another 30 to 60 minutes somewhere warm. Over-proofed dough is slack, sticky and full of large bubbles that pop, and it tears easily. Stretch it gently and bake it straight away, or press it into a tray as a focaccia. Next time, use less yeast, a cooler spot or a shorter proof.
