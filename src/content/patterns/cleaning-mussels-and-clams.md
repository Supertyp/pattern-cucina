## Summary

This pattern is for home cooks preparing live mussels and clams. It describes how to keep them alive until cooking, sort out the dead ones, purge clams of sand and clean and debeard mussels.

| Recommendation | Why? |
| --- | --- |
| Buy from a licensed supplier, keep them in the fridge in a bowl covered with a damp cloth, and cook them within a day. | Shellfish must stay alive until cooked. Sealed in a bag or covered in fresh water, they suffocate and die, and dead shellfish spoil fast. |
| Throw away any with cracked or broken shells, and any open ones that don't close within a minute or two of a firm tap. | A live mussel or clam shuts when disturbed. One that stays open is likely dead and unsafe. |
| Purge clams in cool salted water (about 20 to 35 g salt per litre) for 1 to 2 hours, then lift them out. | In water as salty as the sea, live clams open and filter, pushing out sand. Lifting them leaves the sand behind in the bowl. |
| Scrub mussels and pull off the beard towards the hinge just before cooking. | The beard is how the mussel anchors itself. Pulling it out towards the hinge doesn't tear the mussel, and doing it early shortens its life. |
| Discard any that smell strongly fishy or unpleasant rather than of the sea. | A bad smell means spoilage, which cooking won't fix. |

## Context

This pattern suits mussels, clams (*vongole*), pipis and cockles, bought live for steaming, pasta, soups and risotto. Cooking them is in [Cooking mussels and clams](../cooking-mussels-and-clams/).

It does not suit oysters, scallops, frozen or pre-cooked shellfish, or shellfish you gather yourself. Only gather where your state or local authority says the water is safe, because toxins in shellfish aren't destroyed by cooking.

There's a trade-off with purging. Most farmed mussels and many farmed clams are sold already cleaned and purged, and a long soak can weaken them. Italian cooks traditionally purge clams in salted water anyway, because one gritty clam ruins a dish. This pattern purges clams briefly and skips soaking for mussels. If your fishmonger says the clams are purged, a few thorough rinses may be enough.

## Usage

**Sorting:** tip the shellfish into a colander in the sink. Pick out any with cracked or broken shells, and tap any open ones firmly on the bench. Give them a minute or two to close. Also discard clams that feel unusually heavy, since they may be full of mud.

**Purging clams:** dissolve coarse salt in cool water, using about 20 g per litre at the minimum and up to 35 g, which is the saltiness of seawater. Put the clams in a colander set inside the bowl, so the sand falls below them, and leave them somewhere cool for 1 to 2 hours. Then lift the colander out instead of pouring them through the sandy water, and give them a final rinse under the tap.

**Cleaning mussels:** scrub each shell under cold running water with a stiff brush, and scrape off barnacles with the back of a knife. Grip the beard (the brown, hairy strands coming out between the shells) and pull it firmly towards the hinge end, or cut it off with scissors. Do this no more than an hour or two before cooking.

**Safety:** keep shellfish cold until they go into the pot, and cook them as soon as they're cleaned. After cooking, throw away any that stay firmly shut. Pregnant women, young children, older people and people with weakened immunity should eat shellfish only thoroughly cooked, never raw.
