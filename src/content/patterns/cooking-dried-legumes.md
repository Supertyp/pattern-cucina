## Summary

This pattern is for anyone cooking dried legumes. It describes how to soak, simmer and season beans, chickpeas and lentils so they come out creamy, whole and full of flavour, safely, and when tinned ones are a perfectly good choice.

| Recommendation | Why? |
| --- | --- |
| Soak beans and chickpeas for 8 to 12 hours in three times their volume of cold water, then drain and rinse. Lentils need no soaking. | Soaking rehydrates the beans evenly so they cook in less time and burst less. Lentils are small and thin-skinned enough to cook in 20 to 40 minutes without it. |
| Boil red kidney beans hard in fresh water for at least 10 minutes before simmering, and never cook them from raw in a slow cooker. | Raw kidney beans contain a lectin that causes severe vomiting and diarrhoea. A rolling boil destroys it, but low heat can leave beans more toxic than raw. |
| Cover with fresh water by about 5 cm, add aromatics and a splash of olive oil, and keep the pot at a gentle simmer where the water barely trembles. | Gentle heat cooks the inside before the skins split, and the aromatics flavour the beans and their cooking liquid. |
| Salt the soaking or cooking water from the start (about 10 g per litre), but add tomato, wine or vinegar only once the beans are tender. | Salt seasons the beans right through and helps soften the skins; the old idea that it keeps beans hard has been tested and doesn't hold. Acid does slow softening, so beans cooked with tomato from the start can stay hard. |
| Cook until the beans are creamy right through, cool them in their liquid, and refrigerate within 2 hours. | The liquid keeps them moist and is useful for soups, and quick chilling stops bacteria growing in the starchy pot. |

## Context

This pattern suits dried cannellini, borlotti, chickpeas, broad beans and lentils for soups such as pasta e fagioli, stews, or simply dressed with oil and sage.

It does not suit fresh beans in the pod, which need no soaking, green beans, or pressure-cooker timings, which are much shorter.

There's a trade-off between flavour and convenience. Home-cooked beans have a creamier texture, cost less and give you a flavourful cooking liquid. Tinned beans are ready in seconds and are already safely cooked. This pattern favours cooking from dry for dishes where beans are the star. Tinned are fine for a quick soup or salad: drain and rinse them, then heat them through.

## Usage

**Soaking and boiling:** pick out any stones or shrivelled beans, then soak in a large bowl, as they double in size. For red kidney beans and any bean you're unsure of, start with that 10-minute hard boil. Very old beans may never soften, so buy from a busy shop.

**Simmering:** a few sage leaves and a crushed garlic clove suit cannellini, while rosemary, bay or a piece of celery suits chickpeas and borlotti. Bring the pot up slowly, skim off any foam, then keep it just shivering. Times vary with age and type: beans take about 1 to 2 hours, chickpeas 1½ to 2½ hours, and lentils 20 to 40 minutes.

**Knowing when they're done:** taste five beans, not one. They should be creamy all the way through, with no chalky centre, and squash easily between your fingers. For a soup like pasta e fagioli, blend or mash a third of the beans with some liquid to thicken it, then cook the pasta in it.

**Common mistakes:** boiling hard so the skins burst, adding tomato too early, and cooking kidney beans gently from raw. Many cooks add bicarbonate of soda to the soaking water for chickpeas, about 1 teaspoon per 150 g, especially where the water is hard. It speeds softening, but too much, or adding it to the cooking pot, gives a soapy taste and mushy texture, so rinse well.
