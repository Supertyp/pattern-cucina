## Summary

This pattern is for anyone cooking squid or prawns. It describes the rule of "two minutes or forty": cook them very hot and fast, or slowly and gently until tender, and never in between. It also covers cleaning squid and deveining prawns.

| Recommendation | Why? |
| --- | --- |
| For tender squid, either cook it for 1 to 2 minutes over very high heat or braise it gently for 45 minutes to an hour or more. | Squid's proteins tighten and squeeze out moisture within a few minutes, making it rubbery. Long, gentle cooking then breaks down the connective tissue and it softens again. |
| Cook prawns for 2 to 4 minutes, just until they turn opaque and curl into a loose C. | Prawns have no connective tissue to break down, so they only get tougher. A tight O curl means they're overcooked. |
| Dry squid and prawns well and cook them in small batches in a very hot pan. | Wet seafood in a crowded pan stews in its juices and toughens before it can colour. |
| Score squid tubes lightly in a criss-cross pattern on the inside, or cut them into rings. | Scoring helps the heat reach the flesh quickly and stops the pieces curling up tightly. |
| Devein prawns by cutting along the back and pulling out the dark thread. | The vein is the gut, which can be gritty and look unappealing, though it's safe to eat. |

## Context

This pattern suits squid (*calamari*), cuttlefish (*seppie*), baby octopus and raw prawns (*gamberi*), grilled, pan-fried or fried as in *fritto misto*, or braised with tomato and wine as in *seppie in umido*.

It does not suit cooked prawns, which only need to be warmed in the sauce, or large octopus, which needs a longer, gentler method of its own. For choosing seafood, see [Buying fresh fish and shellfish](../buying-fresh-seafood/).

There's a trade-off between speed and flavour. A fast sear keeps squid sweet and delicate and is ready in minutes, while a slow braise gives deep flavour and a soft texture but takes an hour. This pattern treats both as right. Choose the fast way for grilling, frying and quick pasta sauces, and the slow way for stews and stuffed squid.

## Usage

**Cleaning squid:** hold the body and pull out the head and tentacles. Pull the clear, plastic-like quill from inside the tube, and scrape out anything left inside. Peel off the purple skin and pull off the fins. Cut the tentacles just below the eyes and squeeze out the hard beak at their centre. Rinse and pat dry. Keep the ink sac if you want to colour a risotto or pasta. Use a sharp knife (see [Sharp knife, stable board](../sharp-knife-and-stable-board/)).

**Preparing prawns:** twist off the heads (keep them and the shells for a stock), peel the shell, leaving the tail on if you like. Cut a shallow slit along the back and lift out the vein with the tip of the knife. To keep the shells on for grilling, cut through the shell along the back with scissors to reach the vein. Keep raw prawns cold until the moment you cook them.

**Cooking fast:** heat a heavy pan or grill until it smokes lightly, add a little oil, then the seafood in one layer. Squid turns opaque white within a minute; take it off as soon as it does. Season with salt, lemon, garlic and parsley off the heat, so the garlic doesn't burn. For frying, see [Deep-frying at home](../deep-frying-at-home/).

**Cooking slowly:** soften onion and garlic in oil, add the squid, then a splash of wine, tomatoes and enough liquid to cover. Cover and simmer very gently for 45 minutes to an hour, until a knife slides in easily. If it's still firm, keep going: it will get softer, not tougher. Add peas or potatoes for the last 20 minutes, in the style of a braise (see [Braising meat](../braising-meat/)).
