## Summary

This pattern is for anyone grilling a thick steak, such as a T-bone, rib-eye or sirloin. It describes how to get a deep brown crust and an evenly cooked centre, and how to know when to take it off.

| Recommendation | Why? |
| --- | --- |
| Choose a steak at least 3 cm thick (a *fiorentina* is often 5 cm or more) and pat it dry. | A thick steak can build a crust before the centre overcooks, and a dry surface browns where a wet one steams. |
| Get the grill or griddle pan very hot, with a cooler zone to move the steak to. | The browning reactions that create the crust need a surface well over 150 °C. A cooler zone lets a very thick steak cook through without burning. |
| Salt generously, either at least 40 minutes before or just before it goes on. | Salt draws moisture out, then it's reabsorbed after about 40 minutes. In between, the surface is wet and browns poorly. |
| Use a probe thermometer and take the steak off about 3 to 5 °C below your target (around 50 °C for rare, 54 °C for medium-rare). | Touch and timings vary with the cut and the grill, and the temperature keeps rising while the steak rests. |
| Rest it for 5 to 10 minutes, then slice across the grain. | Resting lets the heat even out and the juices settle, and short fibres make each slice tender. |

## Context

This pattern suits tender whole steaks for grilling: T-bone or porterhouse for *bistecca alla fiorentina*, rib-eye, sirloin and rump, served whole or as *tagliata* (sliced steak on rocket).

It does not suit thin steaks, which cook through before they brown (sear them fast in a pan, see [Browning meat](../browning-meat/)), tough cuts that need braising (see [Braising meat](../braising-meat/)), or minced, rolled or mechanically tenderised meat, which must be cooked to 75 °C.

There's a trade-off between tradition and control. In Florence, the bistecca is cooked over glowing coals, salted after cooking, turned once and served rare, with no thermometer. This pattern adds a thermometer and a cooler zone to take out the guesswork. Follow the Tuscan way if you trust your fire.

## Usage

**Before it goes on:** many cooks take the steak out of the fridge 30 to 60 minutes ahead. It's disputed how much this warms the centre, but keep it under 2 hours. Light a charcoal grill well ahead so the coals are glowing and covered in white ash, or heat a heavy griddle pan for 5 minutes. Oil the meat lightly, not the grill.

**Cooking:** lay the steak on the hottest part and leave it alone until it lifts easily with a dark brown crust, about 3 to 5 minutes. Turn it once or several times: turning often cooks slightly more evenly, turning once is traditional, and both work. For a T-bone, stand it upright on the bone for a few minutes at the end so the meat near the bone cooks. If the outside is done but the centre isn't, move it to the cooler zone or lower the heat.

**Checking doneness:** push the probe into the thickest part from the side, away from the bone, which reads differently. Whole cuts can safely be served rare as long as the outside is well browned, because bacteria are on the surface, not inside (see [Cooking meat by temperature](../cooking-meat-by-temperature/)). Use a clean plate for the cooked steak.

**Resting and serving:** rest the steak on a warm board, loosely covered, for 5 to 10 minutes (see [Resting meat](../resting-meat/)). For tagliata, slice it 1 cm thick across the grain and pour the resting juices over. Finish with flaky salt and good olive oil.
