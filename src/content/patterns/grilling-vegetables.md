## Summary

This pattern is for anyone who wants Italian-style grilled vegetables (*verdure grigliate*) at home. It describes how to cut, grill and dress them so they're marked and tender, then soaked in good oil rather than in burnt fat.

| Recommendation | Why? |
| --- | --- |
| Cut slices of even thickness, about 5 to 8 mm, lengthwise for eggplant and zucchini. | Even slices cook at the same rate, and thin ones soften right through before the outside burns. |
| Heat the grill pan dry for about 5 minutes until very hot, and put the vegetables on dry. | A hot, dry surface sears and marks at once. Oil on a ridged pan smokes and burns, and oiled eggplant soaks it up and turns greasy. |
| Leave each slice alone until it releases by itself, then turn it once (usually 2 to 4 minutes a side). | Moving the slices tears them and stops the stripes forming. A slice that sticks isn't ready yet. |
| Dress them after grilling with extra virgin olive oil, salt, sliced garlic, herbs and a splash of vinegar, and let them sit for at least 30 minutes. | Warm vegetables absorb the dressing, so the oil adds flavour without frying, and the rest lets the garlic and herbs come through. |
| Keep marinated vegetables in the fridge and eat them within 3 to 4 days. | Garlic and herbs in oil are low-acid and airless, which is the condition botulism bacteria like, and cold storage keeps them safe. |

## Context

This pattern suits firm, watery vegetables that slice well: eggplant, zucchini, capsicum (peppers), radicchio, fennel, onions, mushrooms and asparagus, served warm or at room temperature as an antipasto or contorno.

It does not suit root vegetables and potatoes, which need longer, gentler heat (see [Roasting vegetables](../roasting-vegetables/)), or eggplant destined for frying in a parmigiana (see [Preparing eggplant](../preparing-eggplant/)).

There's a trade-off between a dry pan and a lightly oiled one. Grilling dry and dressing afterwards is the traditional Italian home method and gives cleaner, less greasy vegetables. On an outdoor barbecue with wide bars, a very light brush of oil (or salt and oil together just before cooking) helps stop sticking and adds a little browning. This pattern takes the dry side for a grill pan and accepts a thin brush of oil on the barbecue.

## Usage

**Preparing the vegetables:** pat everything dry. Zucchini go straight on. Eggplant can be salted for 30 minutes and dried if it's large and seedy, but young, firm eggplant doesn't need it. Grill whole peppers until blackened all over, put them in a covered bowl for 10 minutes, then peel off the skin and tear them into strips. Quarter radicchio and fennel through the root so the pieces hold together.

**Getting the heat right:** the pan is hot enough when a drop of water skitters and disappears in a second or two. Cook in a single layer and don't crowd, or the vegetables steam. You'll hear a steady sizzle and smell a sweet, slightly smoky charring; if the smell turns acrid and the stripes go black before the slice is soft, turn the heat down. Eggplant is done when the flesh is creamy and translucent all the way through, with no white, spongy centre. Turn the extractor fan on, and use tongs and a dry cloth: a cast-iron grill pan stays dangerously hot for a long time.

**Dressing and serving:** layer the hot slices in a dish, and between layers scatter salt, a few slices of garlic, chopped parsley, mint or basil, or a pinch of dried oregano, then pour over [good extra virgin olive oil](../choosing-extra-virgin-olive-oil/) and a few drops of red wine vinegar or balsamic. They're best after an hour at room temperature. Grilled vegetables keep their place on an [antipasto board](../building-an-antipasto-board/) and make a fine sandwich filling the next day. Bring chilled leftovers back to room temperature before serving, since cold oil turns cloudy and dulls the flavour.
