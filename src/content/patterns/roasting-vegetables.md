## Summary

This pattern is for anyone roasting vegetables in a home oven. It describes how to cut, oil and space them so they brown and caramelise on the outside and turn tender inside, as in Italian roast potatoes with rosemary or a tray of roasted peppers.

| Recommendation | Why? |
| --- | --- |
| Cut vegetables into even pieces, about 3 to 4 cm, and group them by how long they take. | Even pieces finish together. Dense potatoes and pumpkin need longer than peppers, zucchini or onions. |
| Toss them in about 2 tablespoons of olive oil per 500 g, with salt, until every piece glistens. | Oil carries heat into the surface and lets it brown. Dry patches stay pale and leathery, and too much oil makes them greasy. |
| Spread them in a single layer with space between the pieces, using two trays if needed. | Vegetables release steam as they cook. Crowded together, they sit in it and stew instead of browning. |
| Roast at 200 to 220 °C (180 to 200 °C fan), turning them once halfway. | High heat evaporates surface water fast so the sugars can caramelise, while the inside cooks through. |

## Context

This pattern suits potatoes, pumpkin, carrots, onions, fennel, capsicum (peppers), eggplant, zucchini, cauliflower and broccoli, roasted as a *contorno* (side dish) or for an antipasto. Choose what's in season (see [Shopping by season](../shopping-by-season/)).

It does not suit leafy greens (see [Sautéing greens with garlic and chilli](../sauteing-greens-with-garlic/)), vegetables you want charred in stripes on a grill (see [Grilling vegetables](../grilling-vegetables/)), or vegetables for a slow braise.

There's a trade-off between the oven and the stovetop. *Peperonata*, the Italian stew of peppers, onion and tomato, is traditionally cooked slowly in a pan, which gives a soft, saucy, sweet-sour result. Roasting the same vegetables gives more browning and needs less attention, but less sauce. This pattern takes the oven for ease and colour. Choose the pan when you want peperonata's silky texture.

## Usage

**Cutting and grouping:** cut potatoes into wedges or chunks, pumpkin into slices with the skin on, peppers into wide strips and onions and fennel into wedges held together at the root. If you mix vegetables on one tray, put the slow ones in first and add the quick ones 15 to 20 minutes later. A flat cut side touching the hot tray browns best.

**Getting them crisp:** preheat the oven fully, and preheat the tray too for potatoes. For extra-crisp roast potatoes, boil the chunks in salted water for about 8 minutes, drain, let them steam dry and shake them in the pan to rough up the edges before oiling. The fluffy surface crisps in the oven. Add rosemary, unpeeled garlic cloves and a little more oil for the last 15 to 20 minutes, so the herbs don't burn. Everyday olive oil is fine for roasting, and a drizzle of your best one on serving adds fresh flavour (see [Choosing extra virgin olive oil](../choosing-extra-virgin-olive-oil/)).

**Knowing when they're done:** a knife slides in easily, the edges are golden or dark brown in places and the vegetables smell sweet and nutty. Potatoes take 40 to 50 minutes, pumpkin and carrots 30 to 40, peppers, zucchini and eggplant 20 to 30. If they're browning before they're tender, lower the oven by 20 °C. If they're soft but pale, the tray was too crowded or the oven too cool. Taste and add salt, a splash of vinegar or lemon at the end. Cool leftovers and refrigerate them within 2 hours.
