## Summary

This pattern is for anyone serving wine at home who wants it to taste the way the winemaker intended. It describes target temperatures for each style, the simple 20-minute rule for getting there, and how glasses help.

| Recommendation | Why? |
| --- | --- |
| Serve sparkling wine and light, crisp whites cold, about 6 to 8 °C. | Cold keeps the bubbles fine and the acidity fresh. Much colder than that and the aromas are muted. |
| Serve fuller whites and rosato fridge-cool, about 8 to 12 °C. | A little warmth lets their richer aromas and texture show, which very cold temperatures hide. |
| Serve light reds cool, about 12 to 15 °C, and fuller reds slightly cool, about 15 to 18 °C. | "Room temperature" in a modern home is often 22 °C or more, which makes alcohol smell hot and fruit taste jammy. A little cooling fixes it. |
| Use the 20-minute rule: whites out of the fridge 20 minutes before serving, reds into the fridge for 20 minutes. | A domestic fridge runs at about 3 to 5 °C, colder than ideal for most whites, and a warm room makes red too warm. Twenty minutes moves each close to its target. |

## Context

This pattern suits everyday home entertaining with a domestic fridge and no wine cabinet, and it applies to Italian and other wines alike.

It does not suit long-term storage, which needs a steady cool place around 12 to 15 °C away from light, or fortified and sweet wines, whose ideal temperatures vary by style.

There's a trade-off between precision and ease. A thermometer and a wine fridge give exact temperatures, but nobody needs them for a good dinner. This pattern uses rough targets and timing, which get you close enough. Personal taste matters too: if you like your whites very cold or your reds a bit warmer, that's fine, and remember that wine in the glass warms up by a few degrees as you drink.

## Usage

**Getting it cold quickly:** the fastest way to chill a bottle is an ice bucket filled with ice and water, not ice alone, because water touches the whole bottle. A pinch of salt in the water speeds it up further. About 15 to 20 minutes in iced water takes a white from room temperature to cold. The freezer works too, but set a timer for 20 to 30 minutes; a forgotten bottle can freeze and push the cork out or crack.

**Keeping it right through the meal:** put opened whites and sparkling wine back in the fridge or the bucket between pours. Take a red out of a warm sunny spot and keep it on a cool bench. In a hot summer, even reds benefit from a spell in the ice bucket. Plan these steps into your timeline (see [Timing a multi-course meal](../timing-a-multi-course-meal/)).

**What it tastes like when it's right:** too warm, a wine smells sharply of alcohol and tastes heavy. Too cold, it smells of very little and tastes thin. At the right temperature, you can smell fruit and other aromas clearly, and the wine feels fresh rather than hot.

**Glasses:** a tulip-shaped glass with a bowl wider than the rim gathers aromas. Fill it only a third full so the wine has room to breathe, and hold it by the stem so your hand doesn't warm it. One good all-purpose glass is enough for a home table. Pour small measures, keep water glasses filled, and offer non-alcoholic drinks alongside.
