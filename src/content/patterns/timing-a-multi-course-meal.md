## Summary

This pattern is for anyone cooking an Italian meal in several courses. It describes how to plan backwards from the time you want to eat, what to make a day or hours ahead, and how to leave just one dish for the last minute.

| Recommendation | Why? |
| --- | --- |
| Write a timeline backwards from the serving time, listing every dish's start and finish. | Seeing each step on paper shows clashes for the oven or burners before they happen. |
| Make desserts, sauces and braises a day ahead, and antipasti and contorni that are served at room temperature a few hours ahead. | Tiramisù, panna cotta, ragù and braises improve or need hours to set, which frees the day itself for the hot courses. |
| Plan only one last-minute dish per meal, usually the pasta or risotto, and have everything for it measured and ready. | Fresh pasta cooks in 3 minutes and risotto needs about 18 minutes of attention, and you can't do two of those at once. |
| Cool make-ahead food quickly, keep it in the fridge at 5 °C or below, and reheat it until steaming hot. | Bacteria multiply fastest between 5 °C and 60 °C, so time spent warm is what makes cooked food unsafe. |

## Context

This pattern suits dinner parties, holiday meals and Sunday lunches with an antipasto, a primo, a secondo and a dolce (see [Structuring an Italian meal](../structuring-an-italian-meal/)).

It does not suit a single-dish weeknight dinner, where simple [preparation before you cook](../mise-en-place/) is enough, or a buffet, which has its own food safety rules for keeping food out.

There's a trade-off between freshness and ease. Cooking everything at the last minute gives the freshest result but keeps you in the kitchen all night. Making nearly everything ahead lets you sit with your guests, but some dishes, like pasta, risotto and fried food, lose much of their quality when held. This pattern makes ahead whatever holds well and keeps one fresh, last-minute dish as the centrepiece.

## Usage

**The day before:** make dessert ([panna cotta](../setting-panna-cotta/), tiramisù or a crostata), the ragù or braise for the secondo, stocks and besciamella. Cool hot dishes quickly in shallow containers before they go in the fridge. Set the table (see [Setting the table](../setting-the-italian-table/)) and chill white wine.

**The morning and afternoon:** prepare antipasti, grill or roast vegetables, wash and dry salad leaves, grate cheese and do all the chopping and measuring for the last-minute dish. Lay out the serving plates and bowls, and assign each dish its burner or oven shelf. If the secondo needs the oven at a different temperature from a baked primo, put the one that holds better in first.

**Service:** an hour before guests arrive, take meats and cheeses out of the fridge for the antipasto and start reheating braises gently. Put the pasta water on as guests sit down to the antipasto; it takes 15 to 20 minutes to boil. Drop the pasta and finish it in the sauce while the antipasto plates are cleared (see [Cooking pasta al dente](../cooking-pasta-al-dente/)). For risotto, start the rice when guests sit down (see [Adding stock to risotto](../adding-stock-to-risotto/)). Plate the secondo while someone clears the primo, and dress the salad at the last minute. Make the coffee as dessert is served. Food left out at room temperature for up to 2 hours in total can go back in the fridge; over 4 hours, throw it away.
