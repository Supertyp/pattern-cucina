## Summary

This pattern is for anyone making risotto. It describes the *tostatura* (toasting the dry rice in fat before any liquid goes in) and adding the wine, the short step that sets up the rice for even cooking.

| Recommendation | Why? |
| --- | --- |
| Add the dry rice to the softened onion and fat, and stir over medium heat for 2 to 3 minutes. | Every grain gets coated in fat and heated through, which firms the outer layer so it gives up its starch gradually instead of all at once. |
| Stop when the grains look glossy with translucent edges and a white centre, and feel hot to the touch. | That's the sign the grain is heated through. Rice that turns golden or brown has gone too far and tastes bitter. |
| Pour in about 100 ml of dry white wine for 300 g of rice, and stir until the sharp alcohol smell has gone. | Hot rice absorbs the wine's flavour first, and cooking it off removes the harsh edge before the stock goes in. |
| Have the hot stock ready before you start toasting. | Toasting takes only minutes, and the rice should go from wine straight to stock without sitting in a dry, hot pan. |

## Context

This pattern suits every classic risotto, whether it starts with onion in butter or a [soffritto](../building-a-soffritto/).

It does not suit rice that is boiled like pasta, oven-baked rice dishes, or rice for a soup, where you want the starch in the broth rather than kept in the grain.

There's a trade-off between tradition and simplicity. Some chefs toast the rice completely dry, with no fat at all, and add the onion later, while most home cooks toast it in the pan with the onion. Some leave out wine altogether, especially for children or very delicate risottos. This pattern takes the common home method of toasting in fat with the onion, then wine. Choose dry toasting if you're following a chef's recipe that asks for it, and skip the wine whenever you like, adding a first ladle of stock instead.

## Usage

**Getting the heat right:** once the onion is soft and translucent (about 5 to 8 minutes, without colour), raise the heat to medium and tip in the rice. Stir constantly with a wooden spoon so every grain touches the pan and the fat. You'll hear a faint crackle, and the rice smells lightly nutty. The grains turn shiny and the edges go see-through while the centre stays white. Pick one up: it should feel hot, not just warm.

**Adding the wine:** use a dry white you'd drink (see [Cooking with wine](../cooking-with-wine/)). Add it all at once. It will hiss and steam, so keep your face back from the pan. Stir until it's absorbed and the smell of alcohol has faded, about 1 to 2 minutes.

**Common mistakes:** heat too low, so the rice just warms and never toasts; heat too high, so the onion burns or the rice browns; and stopping to fetch the stock while the rice sits dry in a hot pan. If the rice starts to colour, add the wine straight away to stop it.

**What comes next:** as soon as the wine is absorbed, start [adding the stock](../adding-stock-to-risotto/), a ladle at a time. Everything is quick from here, so have your [ingredients ready](../mise-en-place/) before the rice goes in.
