Pattern languages were introduced by the architect Christopher Alexander in *A Pattern Language* (1977) to capture good solutions in building and town design. Software engineers later adopted the idea to share design knowledge, and our sister site [Pattern Life](https://supertyp.github.io/pattern-life/) applies it to everyday life.

Pattern Cucina applies it to Italian food. Italian cooking looks simple, with few ingredients and short methods, but it depends on a set of techniques that recipes rarely explain: how salty the pasta water should be, why the pasta finishes in the pan, how long a dough should ferment, which wine stands up to a tomato sauce. That knowledge usually lives in Italian kitchens and is passed on by watching.

The site covers the whole path, from shopping for ingredients through preparation and cooking to eating and wine pairing. The **patterns** describe the techniques, each in the same structure, so they're easy to find, compare and improve. The **journeys** apply them to well-known dishes, so you learn the techniques by cooking.

The content is drafted with help from AI and checked against the sources listed on each pattern. Corrections from people who cook are very welcome. See the [principles](../principles/) for how it's written, and [contribute](../contribute/) if you'd like to suggest a correction or a new pattern.
