## Techniques, not recipes

A pattern describes a technique that many dishes need, such as salting the pasta water or building a soffritto. It's written so it works for every dish that uses it. Dish-specific advice belongs in a [journey](../journeys/), where each step links to a pattern and says what's different for that dish. A well-known dish may appear in a pattern as a passing example, but never as the basis of the advice.

## Why, not just what

Every recommendation comes with its reason. Knowing that starch in the pasta water is what makes a sauce cling, or that tomato's acidity makes soft red wines taste flat, lets you adapt when the situation is different.

## Tested in practice

Each solution should come from real experience in real kitchens. If a recommendation is only theory, say so.

Patterns on this site are drafted with help from AI, and sometimes that produces advice that's perfectly logical on paper but isn't something a person would actually do. Instead of hiding these, we mark them with an <span class="aidea-inline">AIdea</span> badge in the corner of the recommendation. Take them with a grain of salt, and [tell us](../contribute/) if you spot one we've missed.

## Tradition as a guide

Italian cooking is regional and personal: every family has its own ragù. Patterns explain the traditional way and why it works, then say when it's fine to do it differently. Where Italian cooks disagree, the pattern says so instead of inventing a rule.

## Context matters

No technique works everywhere. Every pattern states when it **applies**, when it doesn't, and the trade-off it makes.

## Safety first

Where a pattern involves heat, hot oil, blades, raw eggs, meat, seafood or leftovers, safety recommendations come first and are never optional. Food safety advice follows Food Standards Australia New Zealand.

## Short and scannable

Lead with the problem and a summary table of 3 to 6 recommendations. Details come after, for readers who want them, followed by real sources to read or watch.

## Linked, not isolated

Patterns refer to related patterns and list the journeys that use them. Together they form a pattern language for Italian cooking.
